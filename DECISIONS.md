# Decisions

Format: Date · Question · Decision · Rationale. Append new entries at the bottom.

## 2026-11-09 · Residual risk: job start between two status reports (SPEC 8.5)

- **Question:** Can a `$J` end up in a just-started job despite the jog gate?
- **Decision:** Yes, the residual risk remains and is accepted. If Makera Studio starts a job in the roughly 100–250 ms between two status reports, one more `$J` can still go out before `P:` is visible in the status.
- **Rationale:** The machine does not acknowledge `$J` and checks no state. Shorter than `SEND_MAX_AGE_MS = 250` ms is not sensible with polling every 100 ms. At most one window (`WINDOW_UM`) can be affected. Also in `WIRING.md` under "Safety".

## 2026-11-09 · New session on stale Idle during Wi-Fi dropouts (SPEC 8.7 c.3)

- **Question:** Is `START_GAP_MS = 200` enough as the gap between session end and status for a restart?
- **Decision:** Yes, with a documented residual risk: with round-trip times above 200 ms, a new session can start on a stale Idle; the overrun is then, in the worst case, about `2 × WINDOW_UM`.
- **Rationale:** A larger gap makes the handwheel noticeably sluggish after every session end. Also in `WIRING.md` under "Safety".

## 2026-11-09 · Foreign commands during a session (SPEC 8.7)

- **Question:** What happens if another client moves or pauses the machine during a handwheel session?
- **Decision:** Residual risk accepted: small foreign movements (deviation below `FOREIGN_TOL_UM = 100` µm) can cause handwheel steps of at most `WINDOW_UM` to be queued behind them. After a hold and resume by another client, queued handwheel steps continue for at most `WINDOW_UM`.
- **Rationale:** Larger foreign movements make the session inconsistent and end it. A tighter tolerance would treat normal measurement deviations as foreign. Also in `WIRING.md` under "Safety".

## 2026-11-09 · Auto-deselect also in `AxisSelector::on_activity` (SPEC 8.2, 8.4)

- **Question:** 8.2 explicitly requires auto-deselect only for `on_press` and `mode()`. Should `on_activity` apply it first as well?
- **Decision:** Yes. `on_activity` first applies auto-deselect and then refreshes the timestamp.
- **Rationale:** Otherwise a rotation after a pause of more than 60 s could revive a forgotten selection if `mode()` was not called in between. Safety over comfort. In normal operation `main.cpp` calls `mode()` beforehand in every tick, so the behavior there is identical.

## 2026-11-09 · Battery divider on the FireBeetle 2 ESP32-UE (SPEC 9.3, H-6)

- **Question:** Does the UE variant have the 1 MΩ/1 MΩ divider on GPIO34 (A2)?
- **Decision:** Yes, factor ×2 stays. H-6 stays open until the diagnostics screen confirms the voltage.
- **Rationale:** User measurement on 2026-11-09: 4.05 V at the battery connector, 1.933 V between A2 and GND. A multimeter with 10 MΩ input resistance in parallel with the lower 1 MΩ gives 4.05 V × 0.909/1.909 ≈ 1.93 V. This matches an unloaded 1:2 divider exactly.

## 2026-11-09 · Button "held" at power-on (SPEC 9.2 step 2)

- **Question:** When does a button count as held at startup?
- **Decision:** Only if it reads as pressed during the entire `BOOT_DEBOUNCE_MS` window.
- **Rationale:** A short glitch (GPIO36/39 errata) must neither trigger the portal nor initialize debouncing incorrectly.

## 2026-11-09 · Empty password field in the portal (SPEC 9.1)

- **Question:** What happens if the Wi-Fi password field is left empty on save?
- **Decision:** If the SSID is unchanged, the stored password is kept. With a new SSID, an empty password is stored (open network).
- **Rationale:** For security reasons the form does not display the stored password. Otherwise it would have to be re-entered on every change of the machine IP.

## 2026-11-09 · Battery measurement already in step 7 (SPEC 12)

- **Question:** Shutdown only comes in step 10. Is the measurement needed earlier?
- **Decision:** `power::measure_battery_mv()` and the 5 s measurement come with step 7 already, shutdown (`Shutdown`) only with step 10.
- **Rationale:** The diagnostics screen is to show the battery voltage for H-6 and H-7.

## 2026-11-09 · Ellipsis in status texts (SPEC 10.1)

- **Question:** The font `u8g2_font_6x10_tf` does not contain "…" (U+2026). How are `WLAN…` (Wi-Fi…) and `Maschine…` (machine…) displayed?
- **Decision:** As `WLAN...` and `Maschine...` with three dots.
- **Rationale:** Same meaning, without a larger font just for one character. The font covers the umlauts in the remaining texts (Latin-1).

## 2026-11-09 · Delivery of buffered commands after connection teardown (SPEC 8.8)

- **Question:** `lwip_close()` closes gracefully. Frames still in lwIP's send queue at teardown continue to be transmitted and can reach the machine after teardown. Can this be prevented?
- **Decision:** No, not with built-in means. The residual risk is accepted and documented.
- **Rationale:** A hard abort (RST) would need `SO_LINGER {1, 0}`. `CONFIG_LWIP_SO_LINGER` is off in the precompiled Arduino-ESP32 3.3 build, however, and a custom sdkconfig would contradict ch. 4. The risk is bounded nonetheless: at most one window (`WINDOW_UM`) is in the queue, because the planner never leaves more open relative to the reported position. If a new session starts after reconnecting before the old frames arrive, the machine travels about `2 × WINDOW_UM` in the worst case. That is the same maximum as for the residual risk "stale Idle" (8.7 c.3). Also in `WIRING.md` under "Safety".
## 2026-11-09 · Flash size in the bootloader header (ticket #16)

- **Question:** On real hardware the bootloader aborts: `partition 2 invalid - offset 0x10000 size 0x640000 exceeds flash chip size 0x400000`. Why?
- **Decision:** `platformio.ini` additionally sets `board_upload.flash_size = 16MB` and `board_upload.maximum_size = 16777216`. SPEC 6.2 is corrected accordingly.
- **Rationale:** The board profile `esp32dev` specifies `upload.flash_size = 4MB`. `board_build.flash_size` does not change the bootloader header. The build in 6.3 succeeded because the error only occurs at boot.

## 2026-11-09 · Optional rotary axis A (ticket #14, user request)

- **Question:** How is the fourth axis requested later by the user implemented? SPEC ch. 14 previously excluded the A axis.
- **Decision (user):** Rotary axis A (Makera rotary module) with its own 4th button including LED ring.
- **Decision (implementation):**
  - Pins (superseded by ticket #21, see below): button A on GPIO35 (A3, input only, external 10 kΩ pull-up), LED A on GPIO18 (SCK). The only remaining free, non-critical pins on the header (16/17 are used by the PSRAM of the R2 variant, 0/2/5/12/15 are strapping pins).
  - Internal unit millidegrees in `mpos_um[3]`/`wpos_um[3]`, so that planner, window and `format_um` still compute in integers unchanged. The A value is never converted with the inch factor (rotary axis; H-10 checks this).
  - Step sizes as for X/Y/Z (0.01°/0.05°/0.1° per detent), window 2000 mdeg = 2°, `F_SCALE_A = "0.500"`.
  - A is exclusive like Z. Optional via the portal checkbox (NVS key `axis_a`, default off). Without activation, `main.cpp` never calls `on_press` for button A.
  - New gate rule 5a: `axis == A` without a 4th value in the status → `NoAxisA` ("Keine A-Achse" (no A axis)).
  - A counts for `session_consistent` and ARRIVE only if the status reports A.
  - The real Z1 always reports 5 values, even without the rotary module (review). The firmware therefore cannot detect whether the module is connected. The portal checkbox is authoritative; rule 5a only protects against incomplete reports. H-10 (c) clarifies what `$J A…` does without the module; until then enable A only with the module.
- **Rationale:** Safety over comfort. With the same step sizes and a window of 2°, the overrun at the workpiece circumference (≈ 2 mm at 57 mm radius) stays in the order of X/Y. Turning quickly through large angles is therefore slow; this is intended. Coarser steps for A would be a later, separate decision.

## 2026-11-09 · Portal credentials on the serial console (ticket #15)

- **Question:** How does one reach the portal if no display is connected?
- **Decision:** In portal mode the firmware additionally outputs SSID, AP password, `http://192.168.4.1` and the reason for the portal (X held, board button, no credentials) over serial (115200 baud). In diagnostics mode all values of the diagnostics screen are also output serially once per second. In normal operation, changes of connection state (with failure reason), lock reason and axis selection as well as alarm lines are logged serially, only on change. Additionally, while online, a statistics line comes every 5 s (status reports, largest gap, loop and display duration). Serial gets a TX buffer of 1024 bytes so that log lines do not block the loop.
- **Rationale:** Anyone who can read the serial output has physical USB access to the device anyway. The password only protects against third parties within radio range.

## 2026-11-09 · Wi-Fi modem sleep off in normal operation (ticket #18)

- **Question:** On the real Z1, status reports arrived with gaps of 280–350 ms, the gate briefly jumped to `NoData`. Is the ESP the cause?
- **Decision:** `WiFi.setSleep(false)` in normal operation. `SEND_MAX_AGE_MS` stays 250 ms.
- **Rationale:** Modem sleep buffers incoming packets until the next beacon. Without it, latency dropped measurably, but the gaps did not disappear entirely (up to ~430 ms at max. 27 ms loop duration). The remainder lies outside the ESP (Wi-Fi or the machine's Wi-Fi module) and is handled with ticket #19. Extra consumption: about 80–100 mA in online operation.

## 2026-11-09 · Hold session on short data loss (ticket #19, user decision)

- **Question:** Remaining gaps between status reports (280–430 ms, outside the ESP) briefly set the gate to `NoData`. Per 8.7 (a) the session ends every time, and jogging stutters every few seconds. How to work around this without weakening the jog gate?
- **Decision (user, option 3):** New step 8.7 a0: on `NoData` with an existing status, age ≤ `NODATA_HOLD_MS = 1000` and unchanged axis, a running session is only paused. No command, detents are discarded, (g) keeps running. All other lock reasons end the session as before.
- **Hardening after review (a1):** Jogging resumes only on a status received at least `START_GAP_MS` after the hold began. Delayed reports delivered in a batch with content from before the gap thus release no command. Costs at most 200 ms of additional stutter.
- **Rejected:** Raising `SEND_MAX_AGE_MS` to ~500 ms. That would have doubled the time window for an unnoticed job start.
- **Rationale:** Nothing goes out during the gap. Afterwards a fresh status (≤ 250 ms) decides via rule 8 as before: job, alarm, state Idle/Run, `session_consistent`. Window and direction lock remain, a session start on a stale status remains excluded. The overrun stays ≤ `WINDOW_UM + REVERSAL_EPS_UM`, because the planner never leaves more than the window open, even after the gap.

## 2026-11-09 · Pin assignment for the single-sided carrier board (ticket #21) (superseded by ticket #25, see below)

- **Question:** With the old assignment, button and LED of X, Y and Z were on different header sides, in an order that cannot be routed single-sided without crossings. Do we change the board (wire jumpers) or the pins?
- **Decision:** The pins. New: button X → GPIO35 (A3), button A → GPIO36 (A0), button Z → GPIO13 (D7, internal pull-up), LED X → GPIO18 (SCK), LED Y → GPIO23 (MOSI), LED Z → GPIO14 (D6), LED A → GPIO4 (D12). Unchanged: button Y (39), encoder (25/26), wheel supply (19), I2C (21/22).
- **Rationale:** The hardware is not yet built, the change costs only `pins.h`. With the new assignment the board needs no jumpers at all. All inputs without internal pull-up (35/36/39) remain on external 10 kΩ pull-ups. `leds.cpp` now determines RTC-capable LED pins at runtime (`rtc_gpio_is_valid_gpio`), so that `pins.h` remains the only pin source. GPIO14 briefly outputs PWM at boot, LED Z then flickers briefly; this is non-critical.

## 2026-11-09 · Carrier board (ticket #20)

- **Question:** What does the single-sided, millable board look like?
- **Decision (user):** FireBeetle in socket headers, cables via JST-XH, milling rules 0.4 mm / 0.4 mm, battery and switch run through the board.
- **Decision (implementation):**
  - Copper only on B.Cu. THT parts from the top, SMD at the bottom.
  - Board generated by script (`hardware/generate_board.py`) instead of by hand, so that it is reproducible and dimensions such as the row spacing remain a parameter.
  - Traces planned by hand without crossings (topology see `hardware/README.md`), autorouter rejected.
  - All pin holes 1.0 mm, so that one drill suffices.
  - No schematic. The netlist is in `WIRING.md` and `hardware/README.md`.
- **Rationale:** A single-sided board without jumpers needs a planned topology; Freerouting could not have avoided jumpers. With 12 components, a schematic would be a second source that can diverge.

## 2026-11-09 · Battery via pogo pins instead of cable (ticket #20, user request)

- **Question:** Should the battery connect to the FireBeetle via the cable J3 → PH2.0 or via pogo pins onto the "Li-ion +/−" pads on the underside?
- **Decision (user):** Pogo pins.
- **Decision (implementation):** Two THT pads P1 (−) and P2 (+, switched) under the FireBeetle. J3 is dropped, because `BAT−` and `BAT_SW` would cross on one layer with both variants. Fallback: solder a cable into P1/P2. The longitudinal position was measured by the user, the transverse one estimated from the photo; the pads are wide enough. H-11 checks both on the printout.
- **Rationale:** No cable in the enclosure. The risk of a brief contact loss under vibration (restart, brownout counter) is known and accepted. Pogo pins with sufficient travel mitigate it.

## 2026-11-09 · FireBeetle row spacing 22.50 mm (ticket #24)

- **Question:** The carrier board was calculated with 22.86 mm (0.9″) row spacing, an assumption. Is it correct?
- **Decision:** No. The official DFRobot footprint DFR0654 (SnapMagic) has ±11.25 mm, i.e. **22.50 mm**. Pin 1 is 11.32 mm below the USB edge. The board is regenerated accordingly.
- **Rationale:** With 22.86 mm the 14 pins of the right row would have been offset by 0.36 mm, and the FireBeetle would not have fit into the sockets. The photo estimate (≈ 22.5 mm) was right, the 0.9″ assumption was not. Lesson: take dimensions from manufacturer data, do not derive them from grid pitches.

## 2026-11-09 · Compact carrier board in landscape format (ticket #25)

- **Question:** The board (66 × 110 mm) is too large for the enclosure. How does it become more compact without losing single-sided, jumper-free millability?
- **Decision (user):** FireBeetle rotated by 90°, USB-C to the left, 1.0 mm beyond the board edge. All connectors in two rows on the lower long side. Each pogo pin 0.5 mm further outward (spacing +1 mm, center unchanged).
- **Decision (implementation):**
  - Board 78 × 66 mm, topology in `hardware/README.md`.
  - New pin assignment so that everything lies on one layer without crossings: button X → GPIO39, button Y → GPIO35, LED X → GPIO25, LED Y → GPIO26, wheel supply → GPIO18, handwheel A → GPIO23, handwheel B → GPIO19. Unchanged: buttons A/Z, LEDs A/Z, I2C, battery ADC, board button.
  - `ENC_PWR` runs under the FireBeetle to the left around RST and reaches R6/Q1 from the left. Reason: the AO3401 sits mirrored on the copper side, where its pins follow G, S, D clockwise. With D at the bottom, the gate is on the left. If the gate signal came from the right, it would inevitably cross 3V3.
  - The lower 3V3 socket feeds only R6/Q1, as net `+3V3_ENC`. It is connected to the upper 3V3 only via the FireBeetle: the display lines lie in between, and a connection on the board would have to cross them.
  - All GND pins of the connectors are on the board's GND bus. GND from the FireBeetle comes via the upper row pin 3. GND pin 4 of the lower row remains unused.
  - `encoder.cpp` now calls `rtc_gpio_*` only for RTC-capable pins, because A/B are now on the digital GPIO23/19.
- **Rationale:** The hardware is not yet built, the pin change costs only `pins.h`. All inputs without internal pull-up (35/36/39) remain on external 10 kΩ pull-ups. The PCNT can read any GPIO. GPIO18 is high-impedance in deep sleep; the 100 kΩ gate pull-up then keeps the wheel off. The LED pins are now all RTC-capable.
- **Open (pogo spacing):** With +1 mm the pogo pins are 3.81 mm apart. The user photo (FireBeetle underside on the 1:1 printout, scale 2.54 mm) gives about 4.2 mm. The deviation of 0.39 mm is checked at H-11 on the printout. If needed, adjust `POGO_SPREAD` in `generate_board.py`.

## 2026-11-09 · Web UI in normal operation: server (ticket #17)

- **Question:** Arduino `WebServer` with `handleClient()` once per loop, or a custom server on lwIP sockets?
- **Decision:** Custom small HTTP server on non-blocking lwIP sockets (`src/webui.cpp`), two connections at once, per pass at most one `accept` and per connection one `recv` or one `send`. Request at most `WEB_REQ_MAX = 2048` bytes, connection at most `WEB_REQ_TIMEOUT_MS = 2000`. Parser and form fields are pure logic in `lib/core/http_parse` and tested natively. The captive portal stays with `WebServer`.
- **Rationale:** `WebServer::handleClient()` (Arduino-ESP32 3.3) reads header lines with a stream timeout and waits for POST data up to `HTTP_MAX_POST_WAIT = 5000` ms with `delay(1)`. A slow or malicious client on the Wi-Fi could thus stall the single loop for seconds (ch. 4, 8.8). In the portal this does not matter, nothing moves there. In normal operation, after this decision the web server can no longer push itself into the tick logic.
- **Known and accepted (review):** If the server closes a connection whose request it has not fully read (400, 413, timeout), lwIP sends an RST instead of a FIN; the browser then shows a connection error instead of the error page. The `Host` header is not checked, so DNS rebinding is possible; a foreign site can thus reach at most the open status page, saving still requires the password.

## 2026-11-09 · Protecting the settings: password field instead of HTTP Basic Auth (ticket #17)

- **Question:** How is saving the settings protected with `ap_pass`?
- **Decision:** A password field "Handrad-Passwort" (handwheel password) in the form, checked on every save (`secret_equals`, without early exit). After a failed attempt, further attempts are locked for `WEB_AUTH_LOCK_MS = 2000` (429). The form itself (`GET /settings`) shows SSID, machine IP, port and axis A without a password, never the passwords. The status page is open.
- **Rationale:** With Basic Auth the browser remembers the credentials and also sends them with forms from foreign sites (CSRF): any web page in the user's browser could then change settings and restart the handwheel. A field that is entered every time is unknown to a foreign site. The lock makes trying all 10⁸ digit sequences hopeless (> 6 years). SSID and machine IP are visible on one's own Wi-Fi anyway. Over HTTP the password travels unencrypted, as with Basic Auth; HTTPS would be disproportionate for a device on the home network. `ap_pass` instead of a separate password, because it already exists, is shown on the display (portal mode) and no new NVS key is needed (9.1).
- **Residual risk (review):** The lock applies to all clients jointly. A device on the Wi-Fi that sends a wrong password every 2 s locks the real user out permanently. This costs only comfort, not safety: settings can still be changed via the captive portal (hold X at power-on). A per-IP lock would be more complex and is not implemented.

## 2026-11-09 · Accepting the settings and restart (ticket #17)

- **Question:** When may the web UI apply settings, and how is the restart done after saving?
- **Decision:** Only with `axis == None` and no active session (`settings_change_allowed`), otherwise 409 and nothing changed. Checked after the password and immediately before saving. Saving, response and restart run in the same loop pass: response with one `send` (a few hundred bytes), close the web socket, `machine::stop()` (graceful close to the machine, no half-open connection there), wait `WEB_RESTART_FLUSH_MS = 300` ms, `esp_restart()`.
- **Port field now strict (portal and web UI):** `parse_port` accepts only digits. `"+2222"` and `"2222abc"` are now rejected; previously the portal accepted them as 2222 via `String::toInt()`. An empty field still yields 2222.
- **Rationale:** With a restart delayed over several passes, an axis could be selected in the meantime and a session started, which the restart would then abort. The 300 ms block the loop deliberately: axis and session are demonstrably off, nothing can be sent. The acceptance rule reads the `axis` value of the last tick; if auto-deselect was just running there, it rejects when in doubt (safety over comfort).

## 2026-11-09 · mDNS name `handrad.local` (ticket #17)

- **Question:** How does one find the web UI when the IP is assigned via DHCP?
- **Decision:** `ESPmDNS` from the Arduino core registers `handrad.local` and the service `_http._tcp`. Additionally, when the web UI starts, the firmware outputs `Web: http://<IP>/ bzw. http://handrad.local/` (Web: http://<IP>/ or http://handrad.local/) serially; the status page shows the IP.
- **Rationale:** `ESPmDNS`, like `WebServer` and `DNSServer`, is part of the core; it is not a third-party library (ch. 4). It responds from the IDF network task, not from our loop, and costs no loop time. Cost: about 36 KB flash and 2 KB RAM (measured). Several handwheels on the same network are out of scope (ch. 14). mDNS is not auto-discovery of the machine (9.1); it only makes the handwheel itself discoverable.

## 2026-11-09 · Content of the status page (ticket #17)

- **Question:** Which unit do the positions show, and which statistics?
- **Decision:** WPos/MPos always in mm with 3 decimal places (A in degrees), even with `inch_mode`; the inch mode appears as `inch_mode` in the JSON. The link statistics are those of the last completed 5 s window of the serial statistics line (initially `null` after a new connection). The web UI never reads the counters back itself.
- **Rationale:** `take_stats()` resets the counters; a second reader would corrupt the serial statistics. mm is the internal unit (µm); a conversion in the browser would be a second rounding rule alongside `format_inch_e4`.

## 2026-11-10 · Screensaver: timings, waking, attention (ticket #28)

- **Question:** When does the OLED go into the screensaver or off, what wakes it, and what happens to the waking input?
- **Decision:**
  - `SCREENSAVER_MS = 60000`, `DISPLAY_OFF_MS = 600000`, `SAVER_MOVE_MS = 5000` (`core_config.h`). Measured from the last input (press edge of any button, including A without activation and the board button, or `counts ≠ 0`) or from the last attention event. Only in normal operation; portal and diagnostics remain unchanged.
  - Never screensaver while an axis is selected or a session is running. This only keeps the display on; the timer keeps running from the last input: after auto-deselect (also 60 s) the screensaver starts immediately. A separate second 60 s period after deselection would gain nothing; the display then only shows "Achse wählen" (select axis).
  - Edges and `counts` are discarded (plus `accumulator.clear()`, **before** step 0 of 8.7) while the display sleeps, in the wake tick, and for `WAKE_SWALLOW_MS = 150` afterwards (review of ticket #28). Anyone waking with two buttons whose edges are a few ms apart thus deterministically achieves nothing; 150 ms is well below a deliberate second press. Because the screensaver only starts with `axis == None`, the gate locks anyway (`AxisNone`); swallowing prevents the waking press from selecting an axis. PCNT counts do not pile up: `take_counts()` runs every tick even in the screensaver.
  - The wheel wakes only from `WAKE_MIN_COUNTS = COUNTS_PER_DETENT / 2` (half a detent), summed over ticks, sum reset after `STEP_RESET_MS` without a count (review of ticket #28). A single glitch or ±1 jitter on A/B therefore does not wake, a real rotation of half a detent does. With the display on, every count still counts as input (otherwise the screensaver could kick in during very slow turning).
  - Attention (wakes without wake window, timer restarted): MachineLink leaves `Online`, `battery.warn()` becomes true, new alarm/error line (`alarm_ms()` changes), machine status becomes `Alarm` or `halted`. The status is observed directly, not the gate: in the screensaver `axis == None`, so the gate returns `AxisNone` (rule 5) and never `Alarm` (rule 7) (review of ticket #28). Not: `Job läuft` (job running)/`Maschine belegt` (machine busy) (a long job would otherwise burn in exactly the static content the screensaver guards against). `Shutdown` itself turns the display on for "Akku leer" (battery empty).
  - `Off` stays `Off` until the next input or attention, even when the idle time overflows after 49.7 days.
  - In the screensaver, redrawing happens only on a position change (one frame of about 23 ms every 5 s instead of 5 per second), in the off state not at all. Randomness: `esp_random()` (hardware RNG).
  - Serial (for operation without display, ticket #15): `Anzeige: Bildschirmschoner` (display: screensaver), `Anzeige: aus` (display: off), `Anzeige: an` (display: on), `Anzeige: geweckt, Eingabe verworfen` (display: woken, input discarded).
- **Rationale:** 60 s matches auto-deselect; after that the display is static anyway. 10 min of screensaver gives time to look at the display before it goes off completely; off saves power (battery) and OLED lifetime. Safety: the screensaver can only remove inputs, never create them. Gate, planner and window (8.5–8.7) are unchanged.

## 2026-11-10 · Web UI closed without response (ticket #29)

- **Question:** Why did curl get `Empty reply from server`, and how will something like this become visible in the future?
- **Cause:** `respond()` stamped the response with `millis()`, while the time check at the end of `webui::loop()` computes with the `now` from the start of the loop pass. If even 1 ms passed between the two (MachineLink, display, building the response), `uint32_t(now - since_ms)` overflows to about 2^32, is ≥ `WEB_REQ_TIMEOUT_MS`, and the connection is closed in the same pass before a byte is sent.
- **Decision:** All timestamps of a connection come from the pass's `now` (`pass_ms`), never from `millis()`. The check lives as `web_conn_expired()` in `lib/core` and computes with a signed difference: a stamp from the future counts as not expired (the budget is 2 s, far below 2^31 ms). Serial `Web: Anfrage verworfen (<Grund> <Zahl>)` (Web: request discarded (<reason> <number>)) for timeout while reading (with bytes > 0) and sending, `recv`/`send`/`accept` errors, peer abort in the middle of the request, and 400/413; at most one line per `WEB_LOG_MIN_MS = 5000`, suppressed lines are counted. A pre-connection without a single byte is closed silently.
- **Rationale:** One time base per pass is the root-cause fix; the robust check additionally protects against future mixing of clocks. The rate limit keeps the loop free (serial buffer 1024 bytes). Gate, planner and window (8.5–8.7) are unchanged; the web UI still sends nothing to the machine.

## 2026-11-10 · Feed rate from turning speed, bundling, window 5 mm XY / 1 mm Z (ticket #32)

- **Question:** On the real Z1 Pro the machine stutters badly when turning faster, at medium speed the display often shows `Begrenzt` (limited). What causes this, and how does it become smooth without weakening jog gate, planner and window?
- **Firmware finding** (github.com/MakeraInc/MakeraZ1Firmware, tag v1.1.3, read only):
  - `$J` (`SimpleShell::jog`, `src/modules/utils/simpleshell/SimpleShell.cpp` l. 1916–1985): `F` is a factor (l. 1944) on the smallest `max_rate` of the moved axes (l. 1962–1972), followed by `delta_move(delta, rate × F)` (l. 1982) and `force_queue()` (l. 1984), i.e. without the 100 ms collection delay of the queue (`src/modules/robot/Conveyor.cpp` l. 76, 187). `F ≤ 0` is silently discarded (`src/modules/robot/Robot.cpp` l. 1744).
  - Multi-axis (XY): the rate applies to the path, i.e. the XYZ length (`Robot.cpp` l. 1564, 1633); each axis is throttled to its `max_rate` (l. 1685). So for XY at 45° each axis moves at rate/√2.
  - Axis A alone: the firmware converts the rate via the rotation radius and, for a small radius, accelerates up to about 10×, at most to the A maximum rate (`Robot.cpp` l. 1646–1666).
  - Planner (`src/modules/robot/Planner.cpp`): the newest block is always planned with exit speed `minimum_planner_speed` (l. 325), default 0 (l. 47). Two blocks on the same straight line are joined without stopping, junction speed `min(nominal_vorher, nominal_jetzt)` (l. 175–203); junction deviation (Z1 Pro: 0.01 mm, `src/configZ1Pro.default` l. 35) only applies at corners. **So `F` may change per command without the machine stopping.**
  - A running block is no longer replanned (`src/modules/robot/Block.cpp` l. 153) and passes on to its successor the exit speed planned at its start (l. 304). **If a block starts while it is the last one in the queue, it ends at standstill**, even if a new `$J` arrives immediately afterwards.
  - **Queue:** `planner_queue_size` default 32 (`Conveyor.cpp` l. 75), not set in `configZ1Pro.default`. If it is full, `Conveyor::queue_head_block` (l. 157–160) waits and calls `ON_IDLE` while doing so. `SerialConsole::on_idle` (`src/modules/communication/SerialConsole.cpp` l. 500–526) keeps reading frames there (`on_serial_char_received`, l. 509): `?` is still answered (l. 523–526), but a console frame (`0xA2`, l. 190–201) is executed immediately via `ON_CONSOLE_LINE_RECEIVED`, i.e. another `$J` **nested** inside the wait of the first one. `Kernel::call_event` does not protect against re-entry (`src/libs/Kernel.cpp` l. 752–764). The inner `$J` writes, via `Planner::append_block`, the same not-yet-queued head block (`Planner.cpp` l. 57, 73) as the waiting outer one, computes from a stale `machine_position` (`Robot.cpp` l. 1750, 1760) and nests the stack deeper. Result: lost or corrupted motion. **A full queue must be ruled out**, not merely unlikely.
  - Maximum rates Z1 Pro (`configZ1Pro.default` l. 439–446): X/Y 1200 mm/min, Z 600 mm/min, A 3600 °/min, acceleration 150 mm/s² (A 360 °/s²).
- **Cause:** With a fixed `F1.000` (20 mm/s) every short segment was finished before the next detent arrived. Every block started as the last one, braked to 0, the machine waited: stop-and-go. In addition, `mpos` lags behind by about 0.4 s because of the 100 ms status interval and the gaps of up to 430 ms; at medium speed this alone already filled the 2 mm window (`Begrenzt`).
- **Decision feed rate:** `F` per command from the turning speed (new `lib/core/feed_rate.h/.cpp`, SPEC 8.7 "Feed rate"): `F_milli = ⌈v × 0.92 × p / MAX_RATE × 1000⌉`, limited to `[F_MIN_MILLI = 20, F_SCALE_*_MILLI]` (XY 1000, Z 500, A 500 as upper limits as before), `p = √2` for XY. `v` = sum of `want = |detents| × step_um` over the last 200 ms (own ring buffer `FeedEstimator` in the planner, measured only during a session, cleared at session start and end).
- **Why lead 0.92 and not above 1** (review, fresh context): A first draft had 1.3. If the machine moves faster than turned, it works off the backlog down to its braking distance `v²/2a` (at 3 mm/s 0.03 mm, less than one block of 0.06 mm). Almost every block then starts as the last one and ends at standstill: 50 Hz judder. With 0.92 the backlog grows by 8 % of the turned distance, at most up to the window, and there are always blocks in the queue. Price: somewhat more overrun, about `0.09 × turning duration + 0.2 s` (derivation SPEC 8.7), always at most the window. The same formula is applied for A; the firmware then usually moves A faster anyway.
- **Decision bundling (SPEC 8.7 e1):** After the first command of a session, detents are collected and sent as one command in the tick `BUNDLE_MS = 40` after the first collected detent (3 ticks = 60 ms of turning per block). Sending is immediate only when no handwheel block can still be queued (machine is certainly standing; this is always the first command, so the session start on a fresh Idle stays unchanged, and a single detent reacts immediately). Time instead of minimum length: a minimum length (e.g. 0.1 mm) would hold back slow detents for arbitrarily long; the time limit delivers blocks that grow with the speed (at 5 mm/s 0.3 mm), and sends the remainder on release at most 40 ms after its first detent. **Send instead of discard on release:** every detent should arrive (detent-exact); the remainder lies on the same tick grid as before and only goes out if all conditions of a command hold.
- **Safety when bundling:** collected detents are discarded on hold (a0/a1), lock, missing status, axis change, every session end and on direction reversal. Sending happens only in a tick that has passed (a), i.e. with gate None, status and the same axis. The window applies to the bundled amount, the collected value is additionally limited to the window. Tests 80–81.
- **Decision upper limit (SPEC 8.7 e2):** at most `JOG_MAX_QUEUED = 12` handwheel blocks that may be queued. Why needed despite bundling: with long slow turning the backlog can reach 5 mm, at 0.4 mm/s that is 12 s, i.e. up to about 200 blocks of 60 ms each. When the limit is reached, collecting continues and is sent later as a longer block. Test 82.
- **Counting (second review):** A first draft counted each block individually as "queued" as long as `mpos` had not reached its end (in its direction). After a reversal `mpos` moves back behind the ends of the old forward blocks; they then counted as phantoms until session end (reviewer's simulation: up to 15 phantoms, one real slot, stop-and-go). And a block after a reversal within the 10 µm band was considered finished immediately if an older status happened to lie on its end. Now: list in send order (the firmware processes in this order); a block is considered finished when a status received at least `START_GAP_MS` after it was sent (same rationale as c.3: its content certainly stems from after the `$J`) shows `mpos` at most `ARRIVE_UM` before its end; then all older ones are finished too. The tolerance `ARRIVE_UM` deliberately accepts that the count is too low (third review): because every sent block is at least `ARRIVE_UM` long (`STEP_UM[0] = 10`, and a block capped below 10 µm by the window is now discarded instead of sent; previously such blocks could drive the deviation up to 2 in a row), it is at most **one** block too few, directly after a reversal at most two (test 89). Without tolerance, a block whose reported end position lies 1–10 µm before the target due to step rounding would remain as a phantom, and a detent after a pause would be collected and discarded in the same tick with the session end (g) (test 87). With 12 of 32 slots there is enough reserve for this. Tests 84–88.
- **Why 12 instead of 16 (second review):** If a new session starts on a stale Idle (residual risk 8.7 c.3), the list is cleared although blocks of the old session may still be queued. 2 × 16 = 32 would be exactly the full firmware queue. With 12 and the counting bound (at most 2 too few per session) it is at most 2 × (12 + 2) = 28 < 32; the rest is reserve for blocks of other clients. Test 85.
- **Assumptions checked (second review):**
  - *A `$J` is at most one planner block.* True: `SimpleShell::jog` → `Robot::delta_move` (`Robot.cpp` l. 1739–1765) calls `append_milestone` directly (l. 1759), not `append_line` (l. 1768 ff., where segmentation via `mm_per_line_segment` happens). `append_milestone` appends exactly one block (l. 1724). If a tiny motion results in no steps, no block is created at all (`Planner.cpp` l. 91–95); then the count is merely too high.
  - *`MPos` is the real axis position, not the target of the last block.* True: `Kernel::get_query_string` (`src/libs/Kernel.cpp` l. 341) takes `Robot::get_current_machine_position` (`Robot.cpp` l. 404–415), which computes from `actuators[]->get_current_position()`, i.e. from the current step counter (`src/libs/StepperMotor.h` l. 46: `current_position_steps / steps_per_mm`). `machine_position` (the target) is not used for this.
- **Lead for A (second review):** The formula with 0.92 applies uniformly to all axes, including A (code and test 55). The firmware then usually moves A faster anyway (see finding above).
- **Rationale of the speed measurement:** Not `StepPolicy::v × step_um`: its window is 500 ms long (too sluggish when starting), has a resolution of only 2 detents/s and multiplies old detents by the *current* step size (up to 10× wrong on a step level change). A separate moving average over `want` in µm directly measures the distance the user requests, independent of the level. `want` instead of `move`: if the window caps, the machine is behind, and that is exactly when `F` should not drop. 200 ms: 10 ticks, fast reaction. Integer and deterministic, time as a parameter, hence testable natively (tests 76–79, 83).
- **Decision window (user):** `WINDOW_UM` X/Y 2000 → **5000**, Z 500 → **1000**, A stays 2000. The user decided this after being told about the longer overrun. Consequences: overrun at most 5.01 mm (XY) or 1.01 mm (Z); `session_consistent` now tolerates 5.1 mm or 1.1 mm; all residual risks "at most one window" (job start, foreign commands, buffered frames) or "about double window" (stale Idle) grow accordingly (up to about 10 mm XY, 2 mm Z).
- **Unchanged:** jog gate (8.5), session start, direction lock, hold on data loss, session end, status interval 100 ms (separate ticket #33).
- **Residual risks and open points:**
  - `MAX_RATE_UM_S` comes from the default configuration. If the user has changed the machine rates, only the speed is off; the upper limits still apply. **Addendum ticket #38:** On the real Z1 Pro the assumption did not hold (3000/3000/1000 instead of 1200/1200/600 mm/min); since then the rates are read via `M203.1` after every connect, the upper limits are absolute speeds (entry 2026-11-11 below).
  - After very long slow turning the overrun can take longer than 3 s (`SESSION_MAX_IDLE_MS`) (distance still at most one window). Then the session ends and the handwheel shows `Maschine belegt` (machine busy) until Idle arrives.
  - Blocks of other clients occupy the same queue. The jog gate protects against a job running at the same time; for small foreign motions the reserve of 32 − 28 = 4 slots remains.
  - It is not entirely without stops: slower than `F_MIN` and for A the machine moves faster than turned; on status gaps (a0) collected detents are discarded. Whether 0.92, 200 ms and 60 ms suit the real machine well is checked by H-16 (with A/B comparison 0.92 versus 1.30); the values live only in `core_config.h`.

## 2026-11-10 · OTA update over Wi-Fi (ticket #35, user decision)

- **Question:** OTA was listed in SPEC ch. 14 as "out of scope". The user wants to flash the firmware over Wi-Fi. How does that work without the machine ever moving unexpectedly?
- **Decision scope (user):** OTA comes into scope, SPEC ch. 14 removed, new section SPEC 9.5. ArduinoOTA (espota), partition table `default_16MB.csv` unchanged (`app0`/`app1` 6.25 MB each, firmware about 1.3 MB).
- **Decision enabling and password:** New checkbox "Update über WLAN erlauben" (allow update over Wi-Fi), NVS key `ota` (bool, default off), in the portal and `/settings` via the shared `settings::append_fields`/`save_form`. Off → `ArduinoOTA.begin()` is never called, port 3232 stays closed. Password = `ap_pass` (handwheel password): it is already the secret for saving the settings; a second one would have to be readable somewhere for the user and would have the same protective effect. ArduinoOTA 3.3.x stores only the SHA-256 and checks via a PBKDF2-HMAC-SHA256 challenge (`ArduinoOTA.cpp` `_onRx`, state `OTA_WAITAUTH`); the password does not travel over the network in plain text. It never appears in the repo: `platformio.ini` reads `--auth=${sysenv.HANDRAD_OTA_PASS}`.
- **Finding ArduinoOTA** (`framework-arduinoespressif32` 3.3.12, `libraries/ArduinoOTA/src/ArduinoOTA.cpp`, read only):
  - `handle()`: if `_state == OTA_RUNUPDATE`, `_runUpdate()` runs first, then a UDP packet is read (`_onRx`). Invitation (`OTA_IDLE` → `OTA_WAITAUTH`, response `AUTH <nonce>`) and password check (`OTA_WAITAUTH` → `OTA_RUNUPDATE`) thus run in separate `handle()` calls, and the transfer at the earliest in the **next** `handle()` call after the check.
  - `_runUpdate()`: `Update.begin()`, then `onStart`, then TCP connection to the computer and the whole transfer in a loop with `delay(1)`; so it blocks `loop()` until the end. After success `Update.end()` (MD5 checked, boot partition switched), `onEnd`, with `setRebootOnSuccess(true)` immediately `ESP.restart()`. All error paths after `onStart` call `onError` (connection, receive, finish; "No Data" and "failed to respond" fall through to `Update.end()` → `OTA_END_ERROR`) and return with `OTA_IDLE`.
  - `end()`: closes the UDP socket and sets `_state = OTA_IDLE`; with `setMdnsEnabled(false)` it leaves mDNS alone. `begin()` after `end()` reopens the port (`_initialized` is reset).
  - `begin()` with mDNS calls `MDNS.begin(hostname)` → `mdns_init()`. The web UI has already done that; a second `mdns_init()` fails (`ESPmDNS.cpp` `begin`). 
- **Decision acceptance and race:** Pure rule `ota_update_allowed(axis, session_active, battery_warn)` in `lib/core/settings_rules` (`settings_change_allowed` and no weak battery), test 90. `ota::step(ota_allowed())` is the last step of `loop_normal()`, the rule is evaluated immediately before it with the state after the tick. If it does not hold, `ArduinoOTA.end()`; if it holds, `begin()` if needed and `handle()`. Thus:
  - `_runUpdate()` only runs in a `handle()` call before which the rule held in the same pass, and no tick runs between check and call (same rationale as for saving the settings, SPEC 9.1 steps 3–5);
  - no login begun in an earlier pass (`OTA_WAITAUTH`/`OTA_RUNUPDATE`) can outlast a lock: every lock resets the state via `end()` and discards waiting UDP packets together with the socket. Without this, a delayed invitation that arrived during a lock would still be processed after deselection and could later trigger a restart;
  - with an axis selected, espota gets no response and aborts ("No response from the ESP"; if the lock came only after login, "No response from device"), that is the rejection.
  - `onStart` checks the rule once more anyway (defense in depth) and rejects everything except `U_FLASH` (no file system update, the SPIFFS partition is not used).
- **Decision at start:** `onStart` ends the session and calls `machine::stop()` (socket closed in an orderly way, afterwards `send_line()` always fails, as with the battery shutdown). The loop then sits in `handle()`, so no tick, no `$J`, no axis selection. Display "Update" with percentage, serial `OTA: …`. `setRebootOnSuccess(false)`: after returning from `handle()`, `ota::step()` **always** restarts once `onStart` has run, on success as well as on error or abort. Reason: after an error, socket and session are already closed; a restart is the simplest safe starting state, and the user sees on the display what happened. Errors **before** `onStart` (wrong password, `Update.begin` fails) change nothing and do not restart, otherwise anyone on the network could make the handwheel restart with wrong passwords.
- **Decision mDNS:** `setMdnsEnabled(false)` and `MDNS.enableArduino(3232, true)` on the existing responder of the web UI (`_arduino._tcp`, `auth_upload=yes`). OTA therefore only starts after `webui::begin()`. The computer resolves `upload_port = handrad.local` itself via mDNS; the IP works just as well.
- **Battery, screensaver, watchdog:**
  - *Battery:* During the transfer (about 10–30 s) the loop does not measure. Therefore the rule accepts no update on `battery.warn()` (below 3400 mV, not on USB without battery). A brownout during writing resets; the boot partition is then not switched, the old firmware starts. A `critical()` cannot occur in `loop_normal()` (leads to shutdown beforehand).
  - *Screensaver:* Does not run during the transfer (no tick). `display::show_ota()` turns a switched-off display back on after drawing (like `show_battery_empty`). Drawing only happens on a changed percentage value and at most at `DISPLAY_MAX_HZ` (one frame about 23 ms).
  - *Watchdog:* `loopTaskWDTEnabled` is `false` by default in the Arduino core (`cores/esp32/main.cpp`), the task watchdog (5 s) only checks the idle task of CPU 0 (`CONFIG_ESP_TASK_WDT_CHECK_IDLE_TASK_CPU0=y`, CPU 1 not). The loop runs on CPU 1, Wi-Fi/lwIP on CPU 0 are not starved, `_runUpdate` yields via `delay(1)`. No intervention needed.
- **Rollback:** `CONFIG_BOOTLOADER_APP_ROLLBACK_ENABLE=y`, but Arduino marks the new firmware as valid immediately in `initArduino()` (`verifyRollbackLater()` = false). Deliberately left like this: a delayed confirmation (e.g. only after "Online") would silently fall back to the old firmware when switching off shortly after the update. A new firmware that does not get onto Wi-Fi needs USB.
- **Residual risks:**
  - The PBKDF2 check (10,000 rounds, estimated 0.5–1 s) blocks the loop once per login attempt, only at `axis == None` without a session (i.e. without moving). H-17 measures the duration (serial `OTA: handle() dauerte … ms` (handle() took … ms)). Beyond `LINK_STATUS_TIMEOUT_MS` the connection to the machine would be torn down once and re-established. A key press during the check can be lost.
  - **Lock after wrong password (review, fresh context):** Without a lock, a client on the LAN without the password could send invitation and response in lockstep and keep the loop almost permanently in PBKDF2. Now after `OTA_AUTH_ERROR` the UDP port stays closed for `OTA_AUTH_LOCK_MS = 5000` (`AuthThrottle` with its own duration, test 91), via the same path as with an axis selected (`end()`, later `begin()`). **Why 5000:** with a check of at most about 1 s, the loop stands for at most about one sixth of the time and never twice in direct succession, so never longer than one check at a stretch (well below `LINK_STATUS_TIMEOUT_MS = 3000` together with the next status). Guessing is slowed to at most one attempt per 5 s (10⁸ possibilities: years). Whoever mistypes only waits 5 s. Longer would bring hardly more protection, only more waiting time. An invitation alone costs nothing (only a nonce), responses with the wrong length are discarded without PBKDF2. Consequence: espota's automatic MD5 second attempt (see below) gets no response, a wrong password results in one check.
  - **Port not bound (review):** `ArduinoOTA.begin()` reports a failed `udp bind` only via `log_e` and then stays silently off. `ota.cpp` first binds the port on a trial basis with the same call (`NetworkUDP::begin(IN6ADDR_ANY, 3232)` with `CONFIG_LWIP_IPV6`), closes it and only then calls `begin()`. If the trial fails, a new attempt at most every `OTA_LISTEN_RETRY_MS = 5000` (like port 80 of the web UI, but rarely enough that sockets are not burned through), reported once on serial.
  - Anyone who knows the handwheel password and is on the same network can install arbitrary firmware (which could also move the machine). Therefore off by default. The 8-digit numeric password is slowed against guessing by the PBKDF2 duration, but not locked.
  - espota (`tools/espota.py`), after a failed SHA-256 attempt, automatically tries again with an MD5 password hash (new invitation). Because of the lock this second invitation gets no response ("Failed to get new challenge for MD5 retry"); on serial once `OTA: abgelehnt (Passwort falsch)` (rejected (wrong password)).

## 2026-11-11 · 3D view of the position with three.js from flash (ticket #37)

- **Question:** How does a 3D view (three.js) get onto the status page without internet, without disturbing the loop, and such that an OTA update brings it along?
- **Decision storage (deviating from the ticket, which planned LittleFS):** No file system. OTA (9.5) only accepts `U_FLASH`; a LittleFS image would need USB, and firmware and page could drift apart. Instead the files lie gzip-compressed as `const uint8_t[]` in the firmware image (`src/web_assets.h`, `.rodata` in DROM, `nm`: 0x3f40…, no RAM). App partition 6.25 MB; firmware 1,284,434 → 1,464,886 bytes (+180,452, of which 178,094 assets). `board_build.embed_files` was rejected: with objcopy the symbol names depend on the path of the source file, which is unwieldy in the Arduino build.
- **Decision three.js:** Version 0.160.0 pinned (npm, published 2023-12-22, MIT), `build/three.module.min.js` (166,230 bytes gzip) and `examples/jsm/controls/OrbitControls.js` (6,521 bytes gzip, import of `'three'` rewritten to `./three-0.160.0.js`, since there is no import map). No own bundling/tree shaking: the `WebGLRenderer` pulls in most of it anyway, a bundler would be another executed piece of third-party code in the build for maybe 40 KB. The npm tarball was downloaded once into an empty directory (integrity sha512 `DLU8lc0z…uDhng==` checked), read only; `tools/make_web_assets.py --three <paket>` checks the SHA-256 of the three files used and writes `web/vendor/*.gz` (deterministic, gzip -9 without timestamp) and `web/vendor/three-LICENSE`. The MIT notice is in the header of `three.module.min.js` and as a comment in the OrbitControls, hence also in the firmware. The 3D view itself is `web/view3d.js` (5.3 KB gzip), path with content hash (`/a/v3d-<8 hex>.js`, `src/web_asset_paths.h`). A new three.js gets a new version path, a changed 3D view a new hash; so after OTA the browser loads the new version without anyone clearing the cache.
- **Decision timeout:** 170 KB do not fit into the previous 2 s budget per response. For the files a progress-based limit applies while sending (`web_send_expired`, test 92): the connection is closed after `WEB_REQ_TIMEOUT_MS = 2000` without one accepted byte or at the latest after `WEB_ASSET_SEND_MAX_MS = 30000` in total. Pages and JSON keep the 2 s (upper limit = `WEB_REQ_TIMEOUT_MS`), reading the request stays at 2 s. So a hanging client occupies a slot for at most 2 s without progress, a slow one for at most 30 s.
- **Loop estimate:** One `lwip_send` takes at most the free send buffer (`CONFIG_LWIP_TCP_SND_BUF_DEFAULT = 5760` bytes) and copies it from the mapped flash; estimated well under 1 ms per call, so two connections under 2 ms per pass, only while loading. Throughput is limited by TCP window and radio link (estimated 0.2–1 s for 178 KB), not by the loop. Measured on the device (H-19 b, `max. Web`, `max. Loop`). The status poll and a `POST /settings` may wait while loading until a slot becomes free (at most `WEB_ASSET_SEND_MAX_MS`); the poll aborts after 3 s, the page briefly shows "Keine Verbindung" (no connection) and tries again. No effect on safety: the web UI never talks to the machine, `settings_allowed` is checked in the pass of saving.

The 3D module is only loaded via `import()` when 3D is selected; if that fails (no WebGL, loading failed), 2D stays with a notice, the choice is reset. For this the status page grows from 12,223 to 13,618 bytes, the target rises from 12 KiB to 14 KiB; the heap per response stays about 0.35 KB (flash tail). Rendering happens only on a new status, while gliding and on interaction; pixel ratio at most 2. Scene, colors and controls: SPEC 9.1.
- **Decision spoilboard top (estimated):** `UI_BOARD_TOP_MPOS_Z_UM = -102000` (= Z minimum of the travel), not measured; the MPos Z depends on the tool length, a fixed value is only an approximation anyway. Applies with `UI_POS_CALIBRATED`, hence the same notice "Plattenlage geschätzt" (board position estimated). (No longer since ticket #40: see there.)

## 2026-11-11 · Read maximum rates at runtime, top speed fixed (ticket #38, user decision)

- **Finding:** Machine configuration of the user's Z1 Pro (firmware 1.1.3.0.1.13) read over Wi-Fi on 2026-11-11, read only: `download /sd/config.txt` and `/sd/config.default` (tool `tools/z1_config_download.py`, protocol like MakeraInc/CarveraController `XMODEM.recv`), cross-checked with `config-get sd <key>`. - **Decision parser:** Only lines beginning with `X:` count as a response; everything else (status, `ok`, output of other clients mirrored by the Wi-Fi module) is ignored. Numbers without sign and exponent, rounded up to µm/s: a rate read too high lowers `cap`, so the speed tends to stay below the top speed. A valid response is also accepted when late or as the response to another client's query: it shows the current values in any case.
- **Decision plausibility range:** X, Y, Z 60 … 10000 mm/min, A 60 … 36000 °/min (`RATE_MIN_UM_S`/`RATE_MAX_UM_S`). ## 2026-11-11 · Soft power: on/off button as an input, battery always on the charger (ticket #49, user decision)

- **Question:** The latching power button sat in the battery's positive lead (J2) in front of the FireBeetle's "Li-ion +" pad. Charger and board supply share that pad, so with the button off the battery was disconnected and did not charge. How can the battery charge with the handwheel switched off?
- **Decision (user):** Soft power. The battery is permanently connected (J1 → pogo P1/P2 → "Li-ion +/−"), the button becomes an input; off = deep sleep.
- **Rejected:**
  - *Schottky diode from the charger output past the switch:* about 0.3 V loss, the battery would only charge to about 3.9 V (≈ 70 % capacity), and the battery reading would be wrong by the diode drop (measurement on the wrong side).
  - *Button on the ESP32's EN (RST) pin:* holding the chip in reset leaves the 3V3 rail on, so the display (same rail) keeps its last frame lit; the switch sense would be inverted (closed = off); and the ESP32 would be cut off without ending the jog session or closing the socket.
- **Decision (implementation):**
  - **GPIO4 (D12, top row pin 12)**, RTC-capable, ext0 wake, internal pull-up, no strapping pin. First GPIO15 (A4) was chosen; with the re-layout (see "Connector rows" below) the on/off connector moved to the right end of the upper row, which only the outermost lane on the right reaches without crossings, and that is top pin 12. Rejected for the button: GPIO0 (LOW at reset = download mode, the handwheel would never start with the button on), GPIO2 (must be LOW/floating for flashing, drives the on-board LED), GPIO12 (HIGH at reset = 1.8 V flash, brick risk with any pull-up), GPIO15 (strapping, now LED Z).
  - **Internal pull-up**, no external 10 kΩ: the RTC pull-up stays active in deep sleep with ext0, the button only matters with an open contact (no current when off), and on one layer there is no 3V3 near the route. Noise: polling with 100 ms debounce, cable of about 10 cm. If H-23 shows spurious off events, a 10 kΩ 0805 to 3V3 is the fallback (parts on hand; needs a route).
  - **Sense:** closed = LOW = on (`POWER_SW_ACTIVE_LOW`). A missing or broken cable reads "off" (fail-safe: the handwheel sleeps).
  - **Connector rows (user requirement, re-layout of the board):** FireBeetle, outline 78 × 66 mm and the four holes unchanged. Lower row (next to the board edge towards the axis buttons): J9 X, J8 Y, J5 Z, J7 A from left to right, like the buttons at x 16/36/56/76 in the enclosure. Upper row: J4 display and J6 handwheel on the left, J2 on/off at the right end, where the button in the right enclosure wall is (the old J7 position). Buttons in the edge row: their leads are the shortest and come from below; the display cable comes from above; the wheel cable runs over the battery and the button plugs anyway. J1 (user request) sits directly above J2 at the right edge (pin 1 at x 66.0, y 31.5): battery and on/off cables both go to the right side of the enclosure, and the battery tracks just run further down the right edge. Left of J2 in the upper row is impossible (the lanes on the right come down there); in the rows the battery lines would have to cross those lanes. Enclosure: free space there, no change needed; the placeholder `P_JST_J1J2` still marks the old place (hardware/README.md).
  - **Pin map rev 2** (crossing-free on one layer; every source must land in the same order as the connectors): buttons X 13 (D7), Y 26 (D3) with internal pull-ups, Z 35 (A3), A 36 (A0) with 10 kΩ; LEDs X 14 (D6), Y 25 (D2), Z 15 (A4), A 12 (D13); on/off 4 (D12); GPIO39 unused, R3 dropped. The bottom-row pins feed X and Y straight down, the lanes on the right (top pins 6, 7, 10, 11, 12, inner to outer) feed Z, A and J2. Only two outputs come from the lanes, so the strapping pins GPIO15 and GPIO12 drive LEDs: an LED to GND can only pull them LOW at reset, which is the safe state for both (GPIO12: internal reset pull-down plus LED = LOW = 3.3 V flash; GPIO15: internal reset pull-up against the LED, either level harmless, LOW only silences the ROM log). In deep sleep they get an RTC pull-down under the hold. The handwheel's 0V comes from FireBeetle bottom pin 4 (net `GND_ENC`), because a GND path from below would cross the X/Y lines.
  - **Labels:** connector names 1.2 mm, pin names 0.8 mm (the earlier renders were unreadable); `export.sh` also writes `fab/handrad-top.png` (top render) and `fab/handrad-top-labels.svg` (2D top view).
  - DRC: no errors, no unconnected items (only the three known silkscreen warnings at J1). No new parts (one 10 kΩ fewer).
  - **Firmware:** `PowerSwitch` (`lib/core/power_switch`, tests 110–112) debounces with `POWER_SW_DEBOUNCE_MS = 100` (longer than the axis buttons: an "off" costs a restart). Boot: at most `POWER_SW_BOOT_MAX_MS = 1000` for a stable level; off → sleep at once without Wi-Fi (USB, after flashing, after a reset); no stable level → normal start. Loop: first debounced off → `planner.end_session`, then `power::shutdown(SwitchOff)`: `machine::stop()` (orderly close), Wi-Fi off, wheel and LEDs off, display power save, RTC GPIOs isolated, ext0 wake on "on". The battery shutdown uses the same function (`BatteryEmpty`, message for 5 s first).
  - **Empty battery:** before, deep sleep without wake source and power-cycling by the switch. Now ext0 waits for the **opposite** level of the button: with the button on it wakes on "off", the boot sees "off" and sleeps waiting for "on"; switching on starts normally and measures the battery again (critical → asleep again). It never restarts by itself and needs off/on to retry, as before. With the button on, the RTC pull-up costs about 75 µA through the closed contact until the user switches off.
  - **OTA:** during the transfer the loop is blocked in `ArduinoOTA.handle()`, the button is not read. The transfer ends (success or error) with the usual restart, the boot finds the button off and sleeps. The machine socket is already closed at `onStart`. Aborting the transfer from the progress callback was rejected: it would add a second, rarely tested exit from the update, for at most about 30 s gained.
- **Residual risk:** "Off" is not a disconnect. The board draws about 0.5 mA asleep (H-5, target < 1 mA): a 3000 mAh battery lasts months; the battery's protection board cuts off at 3.0 V. For storage unplug J1. On USB the board can no longer be power-cycled with the button (it sleeps instead, which is enough to leave diagnostics).
- **Review addendum (fresh context):**
  - **Board revision guard:** NVS key `board_rev`, read before any output is configured (`board_rev_action`, `BoardRevConfirm`, tests 113–114, SPEC 9.2). Firmware for another revision would drive outputs into inputs of this board (e.g. a GPIO into a closed switch to GND). Safe mode drives nothing, shows "Falsche Firmware" and waits for the board button held 3 s (deliberate board change) or for the right firmware over USB. **No OTA in safe mode:** it needs Wi-Fi, mDNS, the web UI and the OTA acceptance rule, i.e. most of normal operation; the USB socket is reachable in the enclosure. After 10 min the chip sleeps without wake source (battery).
  - **Orderly close on power-off:** `machine::stop()` now calls `shutdown(SHUT_RDWR)` before closing, and `power::shutdown` waits `POWER_OFF_FLUSH_MS = 150` before `esp_wifi_stop()`, so the FIN reaches the machine instead of leaving a half-open connection. The session still ends first; no `$J` can follow.
  - **Pin orders:** Pin orders: J9 and J8 are G, G, SIG, LED; J5 is G, G, LED, SIG; J7 is SIG, LED, G, G. J5 cannot match J8/J9: of the lanes on the right only GPIO15 and GPIO12 can drive an LED (GPIO35/36 are input-only), LED Z must therefore be the inner lane GPIO15, and the lanes land on the connector pins in their order. J7 cannot match either: its last pin must be GND, because J2's GND drops straight onto it (any other GND path would cross the A lines).
- **Check:** Tests 110–114; on the device H-9, H-23, A-7, A-10.
